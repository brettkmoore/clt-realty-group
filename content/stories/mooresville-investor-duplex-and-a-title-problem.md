This buyer came to me through a referral from another agent who had found me on social media. He was an out-of-state investor growing a portfolio, and he was specific: renovated or new, nothing he would have to rebuild. Because he was out of state, he was not going to fly down for every showing.

So I flipped the usual order. Most agents show properties and let the buyer do the analysis afterward. I did the underwriting first, pulling area data, comparable sales and likely rental rates before he ever booked a flight. If the numbers held, he would fly in within a day or two to see it in person. His time only got spent on deals that already checked out.

That is how we found this one. A newer construction duplex, built in 2022, laid out like two townhomes side by side. No rehab, no deferred maintenance to inherit. Listed at $520,000. I negotiated it to $507,000, and we went under contract in November with a plan to close before the holidays. He was leaving the country and wanted it wrapped up first.

## Then the title work came back

The seller was a national company that buys and sells property across many states, and the chain of who actually had authority to sign off on the sale was unclear.

Big corporate sellers almost always want you to use their attorney for closing. They will tell you it streamlines everything. Under deadline pressure, with a client trying to close before he leaves the country, the easy move is to go along with that and hope it moves fast. I do not do that. I always advise my clients to use their own attorney, and this is exactly why. Our attorney is the one who caught the title issue in the first place. Then he went back and forth with the seller's local attorney and the seller's corporate attorney until the documentation was right.

If we had relied on the seller's recommended attorney, I do not believe anyone would have been looking out for my client's interest the way his own attorney was.

## The outcome

It took longer. We closed January 16, 2026, well past the holiday deadline my client wanted. But it closed clean, with every signature accounted for, at $507,000. For an investor building a portfolio one property at a time, the numbers matter, and so does knowing the person representing him is looking out for his interest rather than the seller's convenience. When a deal grows a few extra legs, independent representation is the only path that protects the client.
