This property carried more baggage than most listings I take on. The seller had relocated overseas years earlier and had been renting the home out. She had kept up with the big ticket items while she lived there: a newer HVAC system, a new roof, granite counters in the kitchen, neutral paint through most of the house. The bathrooms were a time capsule. Original 1960s pink tile, vintage wallpaper, and wood paneled walls untouched since the house was built.

My first attempt to sell it, in 2025, stalled almost before it started. The tenant did not keep the place clean and insisted on being present for every showing. Buyers do not linger in a home when the person who lives there is standing in the doorway watching them open closets. That listing went nowhere.

## Square one

The tenant was eventually evicted for non-payment of rent. That put us back at square one: an empty rental with dated bathrooms and a seller thousands of miles away who did not want to spend more money on it. When the tenant moved out there was debris and mess left behind. My seller did not want to hire anyone to clean it, and I was not going to let that be the reason this deal stalled a second time. I went in and swept it out myself. Sometimes the job is not strategy at all. It is making the house presentable enough for the photos and showings that get offers written.

## The price

The obvious move on a house with 1960s bathrooms and paneling is to price it to reflect the good updates and hope buyers overlook the dated parts because of everything else that is right. I did the opposite. I priced it low, at $245,000, because of the bathrooms and the paneling.

A house priced to account honestly for its weakest features draws a different buyer than one priced to defend its strengths. It draws people who see the updates as a bonus on top of a good deal, instead of a stretch they have to justify. That is what turned this into a competition. Multiple offers within days. Under contract in three days at $249,936, above asking.

For my seller, it closed out a chapter that had dragged on far longer than it should have. It is one of the deals I am proudest of because it was not glamorous. No renovation, no bidding war gimmick. An honest price and a willingness to do whatever needed doing, including sweeping a floor myself.
