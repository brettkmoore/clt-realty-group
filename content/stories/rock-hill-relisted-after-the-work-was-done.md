Most sellers, and a lot of agents, operate on this assumption: if a house needs work, price it lower and let the buyer do the math. Discount it enough and someone will see the value underneath. In my experience that is not how most buyers think. A lot of buyers cannot see past what is in front of them. They cannot picture a room in a different color, or a half finished bathroom completed, or a small deck turned into somewhere worth spending a summer evening. Pricing a house that needs work low does not move it faster. It just sits at a lower number for buyers who still cannot see past the same problems.

This home was a little too personal for the market. Every room painted its own distinct color, an unfinished half bath, and a backyard pool with a small, underwhelming deck around it. We listed it the first time at $255,000 with none of the updates done. It sat 39 days without an offer. Buyers walked through and what they saw was work. We pulled it off the market.

## The work

Instead of leaning further into a discount, we did the opposite. I advised the sellers to invest in the things that would let a buyer see the finished picture. Repaint everything in one neutral, agreeable color. Finish the half bathroom. Refresh the kitchen with new cabinets and a backsplash. Redo some flooring. And the change that made the biggest difference: extend the small deck into a full two tiered deck spanning the length of the house, wrap a platform around the existing pool, and add a platform for a hot tub. A backyard with an underused pool became an actual outdoor living space. Then we depersonalized and decluttered the inside.

## The relist

We relisted at $290,000 on January 26, 2024. Thirty-five thousand dollars higher than the price that had not worked. It went under contract two days later, at full asking price.

The house did not need a discount. It needed to show its potential instead of asking buyers to imagine it. For sellers ready to trade the city for more land out in the country, that meant getting what the home was worth instead of settling because buyers could not see past unfinished work. Pricing down for a property's flaws rarely gets you a faster sale. Showing buyers the finished version usually does, even when it takes real time and money to get there.
