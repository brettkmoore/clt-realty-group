These buyers came to me wanting what most buyers want: a resale home and a quick move. For a while that was the plan, and that is what we searched for. But inventory in this market has been genuinely tight, and after months of looking, the resale homes that were actually available were not giving them what they needed for their next chapter.

The obvious move at that point is to keep waiting, keep touring whatever comes on, and eventually settle for close enough. I did not want that for them. After about nine months of coming up short, I recommended widening the search to new construction, even though that meant trading quick for a six month build. We visited a handful of builders together until we found the right location and the right plan.

## Why slower was smarter

When you build instead of buying resale, you are not compromising on a two story foyer you do not need or a primary suite in the wrong place. You are choosing every one of those things on purpose. They landed on Eastwood Homes and the Newberry plan: a two story foyer, a downstairs office and guest bedroom with a full bathroom, and a primary suite upstairs with its own loft. Built for $485,476, designed around how they actually live. They gave up speed and got exactly the home they wanted in return.

## The lender decision

Eastwood offered financing through its in-house lender, which is standard for a lot of builders and often comes with incentives. My buyers chose not to use it. They had already found a lender of their own that they trusted, and we respected that. The builder's preferred lender is not always the right lender for every buyer, even when there is an incentive attached, and a buyer should never feel pushed into one.

When the market does not have what a buyer needs, the answer is not to lower expectations. It is to widen the options.
