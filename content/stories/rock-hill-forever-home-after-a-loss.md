This home was supposed to be a forever home. My seller lost her husband unexpectedly, and almost overnight the life she thought she was building here changed completely. Selling was not a financial decision or a lifestyle upgrade. It was one more painful step in closing a chapter she never wanted to close.

There is an assumption in this business that a sale tied to grief has to be slow, complicated or handled at arm's length, and that the kindest thing an agent can do is stay out of the way and let time pass. I do not believe that. When someone is dealing with a loss this significant, the most helpful thing I can do is show up with real compassion, clear communication and zero pressure, and offer steady reassurance instead of a sales timeline.

## The home and the price

It showed like a model. Warm and rich, pops of color against a mostly neutral palette, the primary bedroom on the main floor opening onto a two story family room, an oversized garage, and a backyard that backed to a private wooded area. I listed it on November 8, 2023 at $449,500. Full value, because that is what it was worth, and because my seller deserved a clean, fair outcome rather than a rushed discount born out of grief.

It went under contract at full asking price in 12 days. The buyer and my seller had actually crossed paths before the sale went formal. The buyer stopped by a sale my seller was holding at the home, then came back with her own agent for a private tour. My seller got to meet the person who would be taking care of her home next, and that mattered to her.

## The closing we did not rush

Rather than pushing for the fastest possible close, we went under contract November 20 and did not close until January 4, 2024, carrying the transaction through Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's. That extra time was not a delay. It was what my seller needed. Compassion and efficiency are not opposites. Handled the right way, they work together.

Helping a grieving seller close a chapter with dignity, fairness and real support is not a footnote to this job. For me it is one of the most important parts of it.
