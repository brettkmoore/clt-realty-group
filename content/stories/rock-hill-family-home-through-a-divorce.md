This seller was going through a divorce and selling the home where she had raised her children. Her kids were young adults by then, away at college, and they were not ready for her to let it go. That is a hard thing to sit with as a parent, on top of everything a divorce already asks of someone.

What she told her children has stayed with me. Wherever she landed next would look different, but the feeling of love and warmth that made that house a home would go with her, because she was the one carrying it. I did not say that to make the sale easier. She figured it out on her own, and it changed how the whole transaction felt.

## Where I could have pushed and did not

The inside was already neutral and move-in ready, so the prep was depersonalizing, which she handled. Outside, there was one distinctive detail: a shutter color she had strong feelings about. I recommended repainting it to something more neutral to broaden the home's appeal. She said no.

In a normal transaction I might have made a stronger case. It was a reasonable ask and it probably would have helped. But this was not a normal transaction. This was a woman losing a marriage and a family home in the same season of her life, and the color of her shutters was something she still got to decide for herself. I let it go. An agent focused purely on maximizing appeal keeps pushing every recommendation until the seller agrees. When someone is already giving up so much, the better move is knowing which battles are worth having.

## What it cost her

Nothing. We listed at $360,000 and went under contract in 21 days at $359,900. Compassion does not have to come at the expense of a strong outcome. Sometimes it is exactly what gets you there.

I did not win every recommendation on this listing, and I did not need to. What mattered was giving her room to make her own calls on the things that were hers to decide, and still getting her a fast, strong result.
