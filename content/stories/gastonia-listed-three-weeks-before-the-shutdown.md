This was not my first time with this family. I had sold them this exact home years earlier, so getting the call to sell it was a full circle moment. Their family had grown and they were relocating out of the area. The home was well maintained and did not need repairs, paint or staging. The only prep was the invisible kind: depersonalizing and decluttering so a buyer could picture their own life in it.

I listed it on February 24, 2020. Three weeks later, in-person showings stopped almost overnight.

## The call we made

Nearly every seller who could afford to wait pulled their listing in March and April of 2020. That was the obvious move. Sit tight, let the dust settle, relist when the world made sense again. My sellers did not have that option. Their relocation was happening regardless of what the market did.

So we kept marketing it. We pivoted to virtual tours in what was unknown territory for everyone in real estate at that moment. We adjusted the price to reflect what buyers were actually willing to commit to in the middle of a pandemic, and we let buyers tell us what they wanted from a home right then.

It took longer than a normal sale. Eighty-eight days from list to close. But it worked, and the family made their move on schedule.

## The detail I still think about

My sellers were veterans. The people who bought the home to build their own life in it were veterans too. In the middle of a global crisis, that house found the right next owners.

The plan was not clever. It was to keep going, adapt to whatever the moment required, and trust that the right buyer was still out there. They were. This is a deal I remember for what it took: staying steady and finding a way forward when almost everyone else in the market was standing still.
